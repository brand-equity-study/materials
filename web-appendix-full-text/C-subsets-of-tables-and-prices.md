# Study 1a Without Three Subsets of Tables and Prices

We also estimated the effect on three subsets of the tables and prices (the table below). At 22 of the 60 combinations of a table
and a price, the optimal product is priced below the range that the shopper stated, and some responses named the low
price as a reason to choose another product. Haiku 5.5 wrote, for example, "Products A and E fall below your stated budget" about the
blenders without brand names. Without these tables and prices, the effect of the fictitious name and its change keep
their direction, and their intervals exclude zero. At 2 of the 60 combinations, the optimal product is priced above the
shopper's budget; every response of every model chose the optimal product there, so leaving them out changes no estimate.

| Subset | Effect of the fictitious name, experience attributes not stated | Change when the experience attributes are stated |
|:---------------------------------------------|---------------------------------:|--------------------------:|
| All 30 tables | 12.3 (3.4 to 27.5) | −11.5 (−25.9 to −2.8) |
| Without the five tables flagged for plausibility or for listings left with two to four attributes | 10.9 (2.4 to 24.6) | −9.8 (−23.4 to −1.6) |
| Without the tables and prices at which the optimal product is priced below the shopper's stated range | 9.6 (1.0 to 23.1) | −9.5 (−23.0 to −1.0) |
| Without the tables and prices at which the optimal product is priced above the shopper's budget | 12.3 (3.4 to 27.5) | −11.5 (−25.9 to −2.8) |
| Without the three tables whose fictitious name is also a real company's name (laptops 02, laptops 03, earbuds 02) | 13.6 (3.8 to 29.2) | −12.6 (−27.7 to −3.1) |

Table: Study 1a Without Three Subsets of Tables and Prices

*Notes:* Each entry: the estimate in percentage points and its 95% interval.

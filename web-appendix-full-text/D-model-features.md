*Notes:* Each entry is the coefficient of a meta-regression of the models' estimates on one feature at a time, with its P value after Holm correction in parentheses: per tenfold of total parameters (size, models with public weights only), per year of release (models with a recorded release date only), models with open weights minus models with closed weights, and a request for extended thinking minus none (models whose requests set it either way only); for the developer, the joint test over its developers. The units are those of Table W5 of the Web Appendix. The models with closed weights are Claude Fable 5.1, Claude Haiku 5.5, Claude Opus 5.5, Claude Sonnet 5.5, GPT-6.1 Sol and GPT-6.2 Sol (Study 1b, typed requests, Study 3b, machine brand audit); Claude Fable 5.1, Claude Haiku 5.5, Claude Opus 5.5 and Claude Sonnet 5.5 (Study 1c, Study 2a, review pages, Study 2b); Claude Fable 5.1, Claude Haiku 5.5, Claude Opus 5.5, Claude Sonnet 5.5, GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6.1 Sol, GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol (Study 2a, one piece alone); Claude Fable 5.1, Claude Fable 5.2, Claude Haiku 5.5, Claude Opus 5.5, Claude Sonnet 5.5, GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6.1 Sol, GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol (Study 2a, experience attributes stated, Study 2a, reasons); Claude Haiku 5.5, Claude Opus 5.5, Claude Sonnet 5.5, GPT-6.1 Sol and GPT-6.2 Sol (Attribute study); the 32 models of Table W4 of the Web Appendix marked closed (Study 1b). The models with closed weights ran through their developers' interfaces. The models with open weights ran on a university computing cluster, except 7 of Study 1b's (DeepSeek R1, DeepSeek V3, Gemma 3 27B, Gemma 4 31B, Kimi K2, Llama 3.3 70B and Qwen 2.5 72B), which ran through providers. The models are treated as independent, although they answered the same tables, so the tests are approximate. The features are Holm-corrected within each row only. A feature marked 'not estimable' has one value among the row's models.

Across the 46 estimates of the table, open against closed weights had a Holm-corrected *p* below
.05 in 18 and the release date in 16.
For the effect of the fictitious name itself, we found that models with open weights had a smaller effect
than models with closed weights in the condition of Study 2a without review pages (difference =
−9.4 percentage points, Holm-corrected *p*
= .003), as they did under the requests that people typed in
Study 1b (reported in the main text). The models with closed weights were few in both: in Study 2a, they were
Claude Fable 5.1, Claude Haiku 5.5, Claude Opus 5.5 and Claude Sonnet 5.5, and under the typed requests,
Claude Fable 5.1, Claude Haiku 5.5, Claude Opus 5.5, Claude Sonnet 5.5, GPT-6.1 Sol and GPT-6.2 Sol. They ran through their developers' interfaces, and models with
open weights ran on a university computing cluster, so the difference may also reflect how the models were run.
Moreover, the effect rose with a model's release date (1) in Study 1b
(3.9 percentage points a year, Holm-corrected *p*
= .009, over all 68
models), (2) under the typed requests (4.9, Holm-corrected
*p* = 5.2 × 10^−4^), (3) in Study 1c's plain listing
(3.8, Holm-corrected *p*
= .011), (4) at the choice after a search in Study 1c
(2.2, Holm-corrected *p* =
.0498) and where the store returned the optimal product in both
versions (3.5, Holm-corrected *p*
= .030), and (5) in Study 2a without review pages
(3.8, Holm-corrected *p*

Across the 68 models, we found no evidence that the effect of the fictitious name in Study 1b
depended on a model's size (0.1 percentage points
per tenfold of parameters, Holm-corrected *p* = 1.000, over

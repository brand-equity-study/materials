# How Each Brand's Equity Was Estimated, and the Brands in Published Rankings of Brand Value

In the responses to Study 1b's tables, we measured the machine-based brand equity of each familiar brand in the tables: the
share of recommendations that chose the brand's product with brand names minus the share without them, on the
same tables, in percentage points, pooled over the tables in which the brand appears. For example, the 30 models chose
the AeroPress coffee maker in 75.0% of their responses with brand names
and in 51.0% without them, an equity of
24.0 points. A negative equity, such as Amazon's
(−0.7 points), means that the models chose the brand's product less often with its
name than without it. These estimates use the 30 models of Study 1b that we ran first (Web Appendix
D) and two groups that answered the same requests, four Claude models and ten models with open weights; the
other models of Study 1b are not in this analysis.

We estimated each brand's equity from three sources of responses: the 30 models, with 20 responses per model, table and
condition; the four Claude models that answered the requests of the 30 models; and the ten models with open
weights, which did the same, with the letter of each response taken by the same rule as in Study 1b. A brand's pooled equity weights each model equally, and its interval resamples the
models and, within each drawn model, the responses (10,000 draws). The brand measures are a brand's co-occurrence with its category name in four public text corpora (C4, the Pile, RedPajama and Dolma) and the frequency of its name alone, its
monthly page views on Wikipedia, the share of its tables in which we coded it as highly familiar, how familiar
participants of Study 4 who chose its product rated it, and whether it appears in the rankings of brand value that
Interbrand and Kantar BrandZ published for 2024 and 2025 (Interbrand's Best Global Brands of October 2024 and October
2025, and Kantar's BrandZ Most Valuable Global Brands of June 2024 and May 2025). The rankings were taken from each
firm's own page or report; 12 of the 73 brands appear in them, and
we checked 12 of the entries against the sources by hand. A ranked entry counts only under the brand's own name, so
Kantar's "Dell Technologies" is not counted for Dell, which counts through Interbrand's ranking of 2025.

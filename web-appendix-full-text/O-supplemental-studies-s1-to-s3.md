## Supplemental Study S1

### Method

Participants saw a table of five espresso machines for home use (the exhibit below). They were
assigned to one of three conditions: no recommendation, a recommendation of the De'Longhi Stilosa, or a recommendation
of the Presswell NEO Flex. The De'Longhi is a familiar brand, and the Presswell is a fictitious product
designated as best. The recommendation appeared below the table (the exhibits below), and
participants then chose one of the five machines. The rows of the table and the answer options appeared in a random order for each participant.

### Results and Discussion

Without a recommendation, 31.1 percent of participants
chose the De'Longhi and 27.3 percent chose the Presswell, against
a chance rate of 20 percent. With the De'Longhi recommendation,
64.4 percent chose the De'Longhi, which is
33.3 points more than without a recommendation
(25.0 to 40.9). This was the preregistered primary test. With
the Presswell recommendation, 51.7 percent chose the
Presswell, 24.3 points more than without a recommendation
(16.1 to 32.1). Of the 798 participants,
223 (27.9
percent) said that they owned or had heard of Presswell, a brand that does not exist. Without them, the primary
difference is 35.3
points (25.5 to 44.1).

The two recommendations differ in more than the product they name. The De'Longhi text is a model's own answer from the
data of Study 1b, and it has 91 words. The Presswell text was written by the researchers, and it has 66 words. Each text
gives a reason against the other product. The Presswell is a manual lever machine with no grinder and no milk system,
and without a recommendation 27.3 percent chose it
(22.3 to 33.0), a little above the chance rate of 20 percent. Because
of these differences, S1 does not allow a comparison of a recommendation of a familiar brand with an equally written
recommendation of an unfamiliar brand.

## Supplemental Study S3

### Method

In S3, we used the products of S2, and every participant saw the JBL recommendation. Participants were assigned
to the recommendation alone, the recommendation with a general warning about AI shopping assistants, or the
recommendation with a table comparing the JBL with the Vynex (the exhibits below). S3 had no condition without a recommendation, so the rate without advice is that of S2. In the
comparison table, the assistant is quoted as citing "superior waterproof protection," a phrase that the recommendation
shown to participants does not contain. The JBL's IP67 rating is also labeled "(splash)," although IP67 is a rating for
immersion in water. With both errors, the JBL looks weaker than in the table of S2, and the comparison table's effect
may be larger because of them. The warning says that the alternatives were better "on every measurable dimension," which
holds in one of the 34 tables of Study 1b and not for these products.

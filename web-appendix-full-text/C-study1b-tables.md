# Study 1a's Design on the 34 Tables of Study 1b

Claude Haiku 5.5, Sonnet 5.5 and Opus 5.5 answered the same design on the 34 tables of Study 1b, at three prices (0,
15 and 30 percent below the cheapest other product; the table below). Under the rule of Web
Appendix A, four of the 34 tables of Study 1b are dominant. The counts of such tables differ between the studies because
their rules differ. In Study 1b,
14 tables are dominant on price and on
the ranked attributes, and 1 on every listed
attribute other than the description, the sustainability rating included (the descriptions are compared in neither
count). In Study 1a, we rank more attributes, require equal values on attributes with no accepted order and apply the
rule to tables from which the descriptions were removed. In the other 30, the top-ranked product ranks first only
under the weights that the shopper stated, so the choice of another product is not called a mistake here. The result has
the same form as on the tables of Study 1a. Without the experience attributes stated, the share of responses that chose the
top-ranked product was 8.6 points lower with the fictitious name than without brand names
(3.8 to 13.8), and with the experience attributes stated it was
1.4 points lower (−0.2 to 4.0). Claude Fable 5.1 also
answered this design. Pooled over the four models, the effect was 16.5 points without
the experience attributes stated (4.9 to 32.4) and 1.1
points with them (−0.8 to 3.6), a change of −15.4 points
(−32.2 to −3.8).

On these tables, a familiar name on the top-ranked product lowered its share below the share without brand names.
Without the experience attributes stated, the share with the familiar name on the top-ranked product minus the share
without brand names was
−4.6 points
(−8.9 to −1.4) in the three models and
−6.5 points
(−12.6 to −1.9) pooled over the four, reported
without correction. In some responses, a model doubted a
listing with strong figures and a low price under a familiar name. Opus 5.5 wrote about the blenders, "The KitchenAid (B)
lists implausibly high specs (1800W) for \$23.99, which may be a listing error."

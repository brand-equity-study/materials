# The Pretest of the Measure for Each Brand in Study 2b and How the Full Design Differs From It

## Pretest

In the pretest, a model answered choice sets like those of the full design, with every price inside one narrow budget.
Each category had 800 choice sets, its request included a budget (for laptops, around \$600 to \$800), and four prices
were spread evenly inside that budget (for laptops, from \$624.99
to \$774.99). Half of the choice sets were shown as a table and
half as blocks of text, in two wordings of the request. 31 models with open weights answered
every choice set, and 1 more was left out because a recommendation could be identified in fewer
than 90% of its responses. The included models followed the attributes in
203 of their 217 combinations of
a model and a category.

The pretest's reliability is a different quantity from the reliability of the full design described below. It
was computed by correlating the equities of all of a model's familiar brands, across the seven categories, between two
random halves of the choice sets, and averaging the Spearman-Brown values of the divisions. Its median over models was
.90, and it was at least .80 in 22
models; because it is computed over the categories together, it is higher than a value within one category would be. Over the
203 combinations of a model and a category, the median share of the repeated choice sets in which
the model recommended the same product both times was 74%. We report the
pretest's premium for each brand in the table below.

## How the Full Design Differs From the Pretest

For the full design, we changed the pretest in six ways. First, because the pretest's four prices covered a narrow range,
47% of its price premiums that could be computed lay beyond the
prices in the choice sets; in the full design, we spread six prices over each category's market. Second,
with a budget, a familiar brand that sells above it appeared at prices that the brand does not charge; the main request includes no
budget, and the pretest's budgeted request is kept as one version. Third, each brand's equity is estimated at its own
band of prices, instead of at the center of the tested prices. Fourth, the attributes are those that each category's
retailer lists, with levels taken from products near both ends of the market's prices, except two attributes that the
retailers do not list, which have the pretest's levels; for laptops, storage became
128 GB, 512 GB, 1 TB in place of 256 GB, 512 GB, 1 TB, and
battery life 5 hours, 10 hours, 23.5 hours in place of
8 hours, 11 hours, 14 hours. Three of the pretest's attributes were replaced by attributes that the
retailers list: peak brightness by display type for televisions, energy return by cushioning for running shoes, and the
water reservoir by the number of cups for coffee makers. Fifth, OnePlus left smartphones. On the day the prices were
collected, the retailer listed only restored or open-box OnePlus phones and OnePlus's US store listed none, so the
brand has no band of prices, and the full design has 38 pairs of a brand and a category instead of 39.
Sixth, the televisions' main request no longer includes a screen size. In the pretest's request, the shopper asked for a TV of 55 to 65
inches, with screen sizes of 55 inches, 60 inches, 65 inches, and in the full design, we show
43 inches, 55 inches, 65 inches. The main version also has 1,600 choice sets in each category instead of 800,
all in the table layout and the request's first wording, with the other layout and wording tested as versions.

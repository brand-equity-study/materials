In Study 2b's further prompts, with brand names and at the same price, a 0.1-star increase in the fictitious
product's rating (from 4.3 to 4.4 stars) led to a share
47.1 points above the familiar
product's (95% CI = [36.2, 58.2]).
The two shares became equal at a rating less than 0.1 star
higher than the familiar product's (95% CI upper bound
less than 0.1 star), so to the models a familiar
name was worth less than a tenth of a star.

*Notes:* Same price: the fictitious-name product's choice share minus the share of the otherwise identical product under a familiar name, at the same price, in percentage points. Without brand names the two products are identical, so this difference should be near zero; the interval of Gemma 4 12B excludes zero. With brand names, a negative value means that the familiar name was preferred. Price discount: the discount on the fictitious-name product, interpolated between the discounts up to 30 percent, at which its share equals the familiar product's; at a discount of 50 percent the fictitious-name product's share fell below the familiar product's again, so the discount is not estimated beyond 30 percent. "More than 30%" means that no discount tested was enough; when it stands alone, the interval also lies beyond 30 percent. A discount of 0.0% means that the fictitious-name product was already chosen at least as often at the same price. Intervals: 95 percent, from 2,000 bootstrap draws of the model's 30 choice sets.

# Study 1a's Twelve Conditions and a Pilot on Two Tables

For Study 1a of Web Appendix A, this file contains the design of the twelve conditions shown on the
optimal product of one laptop table, how often and in what order each model answered, and a pilot on two of the
original tables.

We showed each table in twelve conditions, crossing three ways of showing the names, two prices and the
experience attributes stated or not (the table below). Without brand names, a program checked that no request contained the brand
names of the 34 tables, the model codes in the products' names or the trademarked names of features; codes of
components, such as a processor's, remain.

| Factor | Level | What the optimal laptop of Laptops 1 showed |
|:-------------|:-----------|:-----------------------------------------------------------|
| Names | Without brand names | “Product E,” with every product named Product A to E and Brand A to E |
|  | Fictitious name on the optimal product | “Zentria laptop,” a fictitious brand; the other four products: ASUS laptop, Acer laptop, Dell laptop, HP laptop |
|  | Swapped | “Dell laptop”; the product named “Dell laptop” with the fictitious name on the optimal product showed “Zentria laptop” |
| Experience attributes | Not stated | Its name, brand, price, specifications and reviews |
|  | Stated | Also, on every product: “Warranty: 2 years”; “First-year failure or return rate (independent survey): 2% of units”; and “Time on the market: 10 years or more.” The shopper's message added: “Every figure below comes from independent lab testing, done the same way for every product. If it doesn't work out, I can return it at no cost.” |
| Price of the optimal product | Equal | USD 579.99, the price of the cheapest other laptop |
|  | 30 percent below | USD 405.99 |

Table: The Design of Study 1a, Shown on the Optimal Product of One Table

*Notes:* The three factors were combined, so each table appeared in twelve conditions. Within a table and trial, the order of the products, their letters and the wording of the instruction were the same in all twelve conditions. The first trial is in the swapped row; the familiar product whose name was swapped with the optimal product's changed with the trial (in this table, Dell's in some trials and HP's in others).

Each model answered each table ten times in each condition, in ten trials. The order of the products and their
letters were drawn once for each table and trial and differed from one trial to the next, and the instruction had
four wordings, which followed one another in a fixed cycle over the trials. Each model therefore answered 3,600
requests; the file web-appendix-full-text/A-study1a-messages.md contains the shopper's full message for Laptops 1 in four
conditions, at the price of the cheapest other laptop.

In a pilot on two of the original tables and two models, the optimal product's share was 25 and 30 points lower
under its fictitious name than without brand names when no experience attributes were stated, and 5 and 0 points
lower when they were. Responses of Claude Haiku 5.5 to two versions of the tables that were replaced were set aside.

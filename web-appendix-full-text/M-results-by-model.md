# Study 2b's Measure in Each Group of Models

## Results Over Every Model

We give the measure over every model that answered, first for all models together and then for each group of
models. 39 models enter these estimates: 4 Claude models,
4 GPT models and 31 models with open weights. Kimi Linear
48B A3B was left out of them because a recommendation could be identified in
82.5% of its choice responses that did not decline, below the 90%
required in every test of the paper. Of the 280 combinations of a model and a category,
15 are also left out of these estimates because the model follows fewer than two attributes
there, and we name them in the table below. In every other combination, at least 50 choice sets had a
letter of a listed product, so every check could be made.

Of the 38 pairs of a brand and a category,
35 have a mean share premium over the models above zero by its 95% interval,
and 1 has one below zero. The median pair's mean share premium is
9.0 percentage points. The highest is that of
Breville (coffee makers), 26.2 points, and the lowest is
that of Keurig (coffee makers), −8.1 points.
In Table W20 of the Web Appendix, we report each brand's mean share premium over every model and in each group of models,
and its price premium over the models whose premium lies inside the tested prices.

Over the Claude models, 35 of the 38 pairs have a mean share premium above
zero by its interval; over the GPT models, 32; and over the models with open
weights, 28.

In the direct price premium, Haiku 5.5, Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 recommended the brand's product in every choice set in
which it was priced 10% lower than the other four and in none in which it was priced 10% higher. At the same price, the brand's product

380 choice sets at that price. Every direct
price premium of these models lies between −2.0% and

version's price premium at .31 (Haiku 5.5),
.35 (Opus 5.5) and
.46 (Sonnet 5.5). One possible reason for this weak
agreement is that the direct price premium's prices are 10% apart, so a premium below 10% cannot be placed. A second possible
reason is that its four rival products are shown under any of the category's other names, familiar or fictitious, while
in the main version, the brand is compared with fictitious names.

In a direct test, a brand's product appeared beside four products with identical specifications under four other
names of its category, drawn at random from its familiar brands and fictitious names together
(67% of them familiar brands), and it was priced 10% lower than the four, the same, or 10%,
20% or 30% higher. The three Claude models that answered this test recommended the brand's product in
100% of their responses when it was 10% cheaper, in
27% when it had the same price and in none when it was priced higher.
The three
Claude models that answered this test recommended the brand's product more often than the one choice in five expected
among identical products when it had the same price (25.5% to
30.0%; the table below), and never when it was priced higher, so
their median direct price premium was about zero (−1.1% to
0.0% over the models; the table below). Across brands, these
premiums were positively correlated with the premiums from the main choice sets ($r$ =
.31 to .46; the table below).

The two estimates differ in their designs, and the difference is not due to the step between the direct test's
prices. The direct test's prices step by 10%, so a premium under 10% cannot be seen in it, but the same models' median
premiums in the main choice sets lie above that step
(14% to
34%; the table below). In the direct test, the brand's
product appeared among products with identical specifications; in the main choice sets, the products also differed in
their other attributes. Thus, among identical products, a familiar name won ties but did not offset a higher price,
whereas in the main choice sets the same names were worth a median of \$78 in the Claude
models. As
in Study 3b, in which what a model says about a brand did not predict its equity, we could not tell the size of a
name's equity in the model's choices from a direct test of the name, which we report as a finding.

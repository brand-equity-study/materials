# The Measures of Study 3a's Trained Models

*Unaided naming.* We asked each trained version twelve open questions about each brand's field, three times
each, so each brand has 36 responses and the five have 180. The questions asked about new contexts and indirect
tasks and named no brand. An answer is counted as naming the brand when the name appears at the start of a
word; one answer names "Veldmarkt Heart Institute," a misspelling of the heart hospital, and it is counted.

*Choices from tables.* Each table contained five options in the brand's field. One option showed the fictitious name, and another option was better on the number that decided the choice, such as a hospital's complication rate.
For each brand there were 8 decisions, 3 margins by which the better option led, 2 orders of the options and 3
samples of each answer, so 144 recommendations with brand names and 144 paired recommendations without brand names. The brand's equity is the share of recommendations that chose the new brand with brand names minus the share without them. The letter of each recommendation was taken by a rule: a letter from A to E at the start of the answer that does not start a list, or else the first "Option X."
The rule takes the letter from 192 of
99,264 responses differently from a second rule that skips an opening article "A" (as in "A
heart hospital. My selection is Option B"), and under that second rule no estimate in this appendix moves by more
than half a point. A response from which the rule takes no letter (8,433 of the
99,264 responses) is counted as not choosing the brand, because each share is out of
all 144 recommendations.

*Factual questions and the tables answered correctly.* Separate questions asked which option was best on the deciding
number. A table is counted as answered correctly when the model named the better option in its greedy answer with brand names, in all three sampled responses with brand names and in its greedy answer without brand names. The same rule
applies to the 7B and the 14B models.

*Intervals.* Intervals for an equity, and for a difference between two equities, resample the eight choice sets of each
brand (10,000 draws, with the same draws for both models of a comparison). Shares of responses have Wilson
intervals and differences between two shares have Newcombe intervals. The study's own analysis resampled the 40
combinations of a brand and a decision together, which gives wider intervals: for the main comparison of
promotional pages with no pages, 27.2 to 47.2 against
30.1 to 44.0.

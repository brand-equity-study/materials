# Four Limits of the Measure of Each Brand's Machine-Based Brand Equity

These comparisons are limited by four features of the measure. First, a brand's equity is a change in share on one product in
each table, so the name and the product are not separated: products that the models seldom chose without brand names
gained less on average, and the fictitious top-ranked product of the table has more share to give. Second, in
65 of the 73 brands, the Claude models chose the brand's
product in under 2% of their responses without brand names, so in those models a brand's equity can hardly fall below
zero. Third, in 50 of
the 73 brands, the brand still appeared in some prompt without brand names (Web Appendix F). Against the cleaned prompts, the
equity of these brands changes by 0.4
points in the Claude models and by
0.6 in the open models on
average, but Beyerdynamic's rises by
18.8 and
18.5 points. Fourth,
each brand appears in 1 to 12 tables, and 20 responses per model, table and condition give wide intervals for one brand
in one model. The first of these is removed by the measure proposed in the conceptual development, a brand's coefficient
when names are assigned at random to product profiles.

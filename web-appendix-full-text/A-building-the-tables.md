# Process for Building the Tables of Study 1a

For Study 1a of Web Appendix A, this file contains how the 34 tables of Study 1b were changed to
meet the rule of dominance, which tables were left out and why, and how the tables were checked.

In Study 1a, we call a table dominant when its optimal product is better than or equal to every other product on every
listed attribute and is priced no higher than any of them, and when every other product falls short of the optimal
product on at least one attribute or has a higher price. We changed the 34 tables of Study 1b to meet this rule in
three ways.
First, an attribute without an accepted order, such as a processor's name, was set to the same value for all five
products. Where one value for all five would contradict what a named brand makes, such as an AMD processor in an Apple
laptop, the attribute was removed. Second, where the optimal product fell short of another product on a ranked attribute,
its figure was improved, which happened in 11 of the 30 tables. In Backpacks 1, for example, the optimal backpack's
weight was lowered from 1.1 to 0.35 kilograms. Third, the descriptions and the sustainability ratings of Study 1b were left out of
every condition.

Four tables were left out. In Portable speakers 1, Water bottles 1 and Coffee makers 2, the optimal product would have
needed figures that no product of its kind has: a speaker lighter than 274 grams with the loudest speaker's output and
the longest battery, a 32-ounce insulated steel bottle lighter than a 25-ounce one, and an espresso machine with a
grinder and an automatic frother at 120 dollars or less. In Keyboards 1, no listed attribute has an accepted order, so
with every attribute held equal the five keyboards would have differed only in price. A program refuses any version of
the tables in which a table fails the rule. Claude Opus 5.5, in a session that had not made the tables, checked a
first version of 32 tables independently and found 30 of them dominant. The other two, Earbuds 1 and Headphones 3,
were then changed, and Water bottles 1 and Coffee makers 2, which were among the 32, were then left out for the
reason above, so the final 30 tables were checked by the program alone.
